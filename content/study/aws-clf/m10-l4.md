This lesson ties security and governance together: how you actually **show** a workload is compliant on AWS.

## Remember the split

- **AWS** provides **certified infrastructure**: the reports in **AWS Artifact** prove it.
- **You** configure **your workload** to meet the regulations that apply to you: GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS.

## Data residency

Many rules say where data may live (e.g. "UK or EU only"). On AWS, **you choose the Region**, and **AWS won't move your data out of it**. You can enforce this further with SCPs that block other Regions.

## The compliance toolkit

Use these together:

| Need | Service |
|---|---|
| A record of **who did what** (audit trail) | **AWS CloudTrail** |
| Proof resources are **configured correctly**, and the history of changes | **AWS Config** (with Config rules) |
| One place for **security findings** and best-practice checks | **AWS Security Hub** |
| **Evidence** collected continuously for auditors | **AWS Audit Manager** |
| AWS's own **compliance reports** | **AWS Artifact** |
| **Encryption** of sensitive data | **AWS KMS** |

## Worked scenario

> Before an audit, a company must show that all S3 buckets were encrypted for the whole year, and who changed any bucket settings.

→ **AWS Config** (configuration compliance and history) + **AWS CloudTrail** (who made changes) + **Audit Manager** to gather the evidence.

## Check yourself

1. How do you make sure data stays in the UK on AWS?
2. Which two services together show what changed in a resource and who changed it?

### Answers

1. Use a UK Region (London, `eu-west-2`); AWS doesn't move data out of the Region you choose. SCPs can block other Regions.
2. AWS Config (what changed) and AWS CloudTrail (who did it).
