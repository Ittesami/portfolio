Many industries must prove their IT meets standards (health data, card payments, privacy laws). AWS gets its infrastructure **audited** against these standards and gives you the **evidence**, but **your workload's compliance is still your job**.

## AWS Artifact

A **self-service portal** in the console where you can:

- **download AWS compliance reports**: SOC reports, ISO certifications, PCI DSS attestations;
- **review and accept agreements** with AWS, such as the **BAA** (Business Associate Addendum, for HIPAA) or the GDPR data processing addendum.

Keywords: "**download compliance reports**", "auditor needs AWS's SOC report", "accept an agreement" → **AWS Artifact**.

## AWS compliance programmes

AWS is audited against many standards, including **ISO 27001**, **SOC 1/2/3**, **PCI DSS**, **HIPAA**-eligible services and **GDPR**. The AWS Compliance Center (Customer Compliance Center) explains what's covered.

## The important catch

AWS being certified **doesn't make your application compliant**. Following the shared responsibility model:

- AWS proves its **infrastructure** meets the standard (the reports in Artifact).
- **You** must configure **your workload** to meet it: encryption, access control, logging, choosing the right Region.

## Worked scenario

> An auditor asks for evidence that AWS's data centres meet PCI DSS. → Download the report from **AWS Artifact**.

## Check yourself

1. Where do you download AWS's SOC and ISO reports?
2. If AWS is PCI DSS compliant, is your payment application automatically compliant?

### Answers

1. AWS Artifact.
2. No: AWS covers its infrastructure; you're responsible for making your own workload compliant.
