AWS pricing follows a few simple principles. Understand them and most billing questions answer themselves.

## Three pricing principles

1. **Pay as you go:** pay only for what you use, with no long-term contracts or upfront costs (unless you choose them).
2. **Pay less when you reserve:** commit to 1 or 3 years with **Savings Plans** or **Reserved Instances** for big discounts.
3. **Pay less per unit when you use more:** **volume-tiered** pricing. For example, S3 gets cheaper per GB as you store more.

## Three main cost drivers

| Driver | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Compute** | EC2 instance hours, Lambda requests and run time |
| **Storage** | GB stored in S3, EBS volume size |
| **Outbound data transfer** | Data sent **out** to the internet |

### Data transfer, specifically

- Data transfer **IN** to AWS from the internet: **free**.
- Data **OUT** to the internet: **charged**.
- Between AZs or between Regions: usually **charged**.

## Services that are free themselves

You pay only for the resources they create:

- **IAM**
- **CloudFormation**
- **Auto Scaling**
- **Elastic Beanstalk**
- **VPC** (the basic VPC itself; things like NAT gateways cost)
- **AWS Organizations** and consolidated billing

## Worked scenarios

> Which costs money: uploading 1 TB to S3, or downloading 1 TB from S3 to the internet? → **Downloading** (data out). Uploading (in) is free.

> A company stores more and more data in S3 and the price per GB drops. → **Volume-based (tiered) pricing**.

## Check yourself

1. Name the three pricing principles.
2. Is data transfer into AWS charged?
3. Name two AWS services that are free to use (you pay only for the resources they create).

### Answers

1. Pay as you go; pay less when you reserve; pay less per unit when you use more.
2. No, inbound data transfer is free; outbound to the internet is charged.
3. Any two of: IAM, CloudFormation, Auto Scaling, Elastic Beanstalk, Organizations/consolidated billing, basic VPC.
