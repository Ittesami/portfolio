A VPC is private by default. These components control how traffic gets **in and out**: to the internet, to your office data centre, and between VPCs.

## To and from the internet

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Internet gateway (IGW)** | Lets public internet traffic in and out of a VPC. Attach it to the VPC; public subnets route to it |
| **NAT gateway** | Lets instances in a **private** subnet reach the internet (e.g. to download updates) **without** being reachable from the internet |

Analogy: the internet gateway is the front door. The NAT gateway is a one-way door: staff inside can go out and come back, but nobody from outside can walk in.

## To your own data centre (hybrid)

| Connection | How it works | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AWS Site-to-Site VPN** | An **encrypted** tunnel from your data centre to your VPC **over the public internet** | **Quick and cheap** to set up | Shares the internet, so performance varies |
| **AWS Direct Connect** | A **dedicated private physical connection** from your data centre to AWS; **doesn't use the internet** | **Consistent**, high bandwidth, lower latency, can cut data-transfer costs | Takes **weeks** to set up, costs more |
| **AWS Client VPN** | Lets **individual users** (e.g. remote workers) connect securely | Per-person access | Not for whole sites |

The **virtual private gateway** is the VPC-side endpoint of a Site-to-Site VPN.

> "Private, dedicated, consistent performance, not over the internet" → **Direct Connect**.
> "Encrypted connection, set up quickly, over the internet" → **Site-to-Site VPN**.

## Between VPCs

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **VPC peering** | Connects **two** VPCs privately. One-to-one, and **not transitive** (A↔B and B↔C does **not** give A↔C) |
| **AWS Transit Gateway** | A central **hub** connecting **many** VPCs and on-premises networks (hub and spoke) |

Many VPCs and a mesh of peering connections getting messy → **Transit Gateway**.

## To AWS services, privately

**AWS PrivateLink / VPC endpoints** let resources in your VPC reach AWS services (like S3) or other VPCs' services **privately**, without the traffic going over the internet.

## Worked scenarios

> Servers in a private subnet must download software patches from the internet but must not accept connections from it. → **NAT gateway**.

> A bank needs a private, consistent, high-bandwidth link from its data centre to AWS. → **AWS Direct Connect**.

## Check yourself

1. What's the main difference between Site-to-Site VPN and Direct Connect?
2. VPC A is peered with VPC B, and B with C. Can A talk to C?
3. A company has 50 VPCs that all need to connect to each other and to its office. What should it use?

### Answers

1. Site-to-Site VPN is an encrypted tunnel over the public internet (quick, cheap); Direct Connect is a dedicated private connection that doesn't use the internet (consistent, but takes weeks).
2. No: VPC peering isn't transitive.
3. AWS Transit Gateway.
