An **Amazon VPC (Virtual Private Cloud)** is your own **logically isolated private network** inside AWS. You decide its IP address range, how it's divided into subnets, and how traffic flows in and out. Every EC2 instance, database and load balancer lives inside a VPC.

Think of AWS as a city; your VPC is your fenced-off plot of land in it.

## Subnets

A **subnet** is a section of a VPC, and **each subnet lives in exactly one Availability Zone**.

| Subnet | Has a route to the internet? | Put here |
|---|---|---|
| **Public subnet** | Yes, through an **internet gateway** | Web servers, load balancers: things the internet must reach |
| **Private subnet** | No direct route | Databases, back-end servers: things that should **never** be reachable from the internet |

```
VPC 10.0.0.0/16
 ├── AZ a: public subnet 10.0.1.0/24  [load balancer]
 │         private subnet 10.0.2.0/24 [app server][database]
 └── AZ b: public subnet 10.0.3.0/24  [load balancer]
           private subnet 10.0.4.0/24 [app server][database replica]
```

Using public and private subnets in **two AZs** like this is the classic highly available, secure layout.

## Route tables

Each subnet has a **route table**: rules saying where its traffic goes. A subnet is "public" precisely because its route table sends internet-bound traffic (`0.0.0.0/0`) to the internet gateway.

## Why this design matters

- **Security:** databases in private subnets can't be reached from the internet at all, even if someone misconfigures a password.
- **Availability:** spreading subnets across AZs means one AZ failing doesn't stop the application.

## Exam traps

- A subnet is in **one AZ** (a VPC spans all the AZs of its Region).
- "Public" vs "private" is about the **route to the internet gateway**, not about a setting called "public".
- A VPC is **regional**: it doesn't span Regions.

## Check yourself

1. Where should a company's database servers go: a public or a private subnet?
2. How many Availability Zones can one subnet span?
3. What makes a subnet "public"?

### Answers

1. A private subnet.
2. One: a subnet lives in a single AZ.
3. Its route table has a route to an internet gateway.
