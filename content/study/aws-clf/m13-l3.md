**Disaster recovery (DR)** is how you get running again after a major failure, like a whole Region going down. The strategies trade **cost** against **how fast you recover** and **how much data you lose**.

## Two numbers to know

- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective):** how long you can be **down**. "We must be back within 1 hour."
- **RPO (Recovery Point Objective):** how much **data** (measured in time) you can afford to **lose**. "We can lose at most 5 minutes of data."

```
           disaster
              │
  last backup │                     recovered
──────●───────✕─────────────────────●──────→ time
      ←─ RPO ─→←──────── RTO ───────→
   (data lost)      (time down)
```

Smaller RTO and RPO = faster recovery and less data loss = **more expensive**.

## The four strategies (cheapest/slowest → most expensive/fastest)

| Strategy | How it works | RTO / RPO | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Backup and restore** | Back up data to AWS; **rebuild everything** after a disaster | **Hours** | **Lowest** |
| **Pilot light** | Only the **core** (e.g. a database replica) is always running; **scale up** the rest on failover | **Tens of minutes** | Low |
| **Warm standby** | A **scaled-down but fully working** copy is always running; scale it up on failover | **Minutes** | Higher |
| **Multi-site active/active** | **Full production in two or more Regions** at the same time | **Near zero** | **Highest** |

Memory picture: a **pilot light** on a gas boiler is a tiny flame always on, ready to light the full burner. A **warm standby** is the whole boiler on low. **Active/active** is two boilers on full.

## Matching the question

| The question says… | Strategy |
|---|---|
| "lowest cost", "recovery in hours is acceptable" | **Backup and restore** |
| "core components always running", "keep the database replicated" | **Pilot light** |
| "scaled-down copy of the full environment always running" | **Warm standby** |
| "no downtime", "near-zero RTO and RPO", "full capacity in two Regions" | **Multi-site active/active** |

## Check yourself

1. What does RPO measure?
2. A company can tolerate several hours of downtime and wants the lowest cost. Which strategy?
3. In which strategy is a smaller, fully functional copy of production always running?

### Answers

1. The maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time (e.g. 5 minutes of data).
2. Backup and restore.
3. Warm standby.
